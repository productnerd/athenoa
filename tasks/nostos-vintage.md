# Nostos: vintage covers (1900 to 1930) for the Bookshelf

A second set of covers to compare with the first editions in `src/data/nostos-editions.json`. The data is in `src/data/nostos-vintage.json` (same keys, `publisher` in place of `printer`, plus `local_file`). Order follows `library.json`.

**15 of 18 books have a qualifying cover. 3 have only a nearest option (Timaeus, Cratylus, Orphic Hymns).**

- Every image is the front cover of a cloth or board binding, checked by eye. None is a title page or a spine.
- The files are in `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-mariap-projects-athenoa/3feb8c8b-524b-4672-bbea-a859f0e4cd93/scratchpad/vintage-dl/`, named `<book id>.jpg`, resized to 1200 px on the long side. **Total 5.7 MB.**
- All come from Internet Archive scans of library copies. I read each licence from the item's own record: "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT" (the record's copyright-status field), a Public Domain Mark, or the Commons file page of the same scan. No licence is NC or ND.
- I checked the printing year of each copy (title page or its back) wherever the scan's text allowed. That ruled out several Loebs whose covers or green dust jackets turned out to be 1937 to 1990s reprints.

## No qualifying cover

- **Timaeus.** The only Timaeus cover with a stated licence is Burnet's Oxford text, and this copy was printed by John Johnson, the Oxford printer from 1925 to 1946, so it may be from after 1930. The Loeb Timaeus (1929) survives only as Internet Archive copies whose records give no licence, or as modern reprints in the green jacket.
- **Cratylus.** The Teubner cover is the best-looking cover in the set, but its record (PIMS library, Toronto) has no rights field. The Oxford text, vol. 1, has a stated licence but the same post-1925 printing problem.
- **Orphic Hymns.** I found no 1900 to 1930 edition of the Hymns. Shown is Taylor's 1896 reprint (Dobell), whose record states no licence. Kern's *Orphicorum fragmenta* (1922) qualifies but leaves the Hymns out.

## Worth a second look

- **Only a few covers are decorated.** Most library copies on the Internet Archive are plain cloth with barcodes. The richest here are the four Teubner covers (Phaedrus, Cratylus, Ethics, Politics), the Golden Verses, the Odyssey, the Phaedo and the Republic; the Symposium, Heraclitus and Plotinus covers carry a stamped or printed title. The Delphi, Meno and Proclus covers are plain boards, kept because nothing better qualified.
- **Not all are translations, and some are not editions.** Three Teubner covers are Greek texts. Heraclitus is Lassalle's German study, which collects the fragments. Parmenides is Nestle's German anthology. Plainer English alternatives that qualify are named in the JSON notes (Burnet's *Early Greek Philosophy*, 1908, for Heraclitus).
- **Google scans** (Phaedrus, Parmenides, Politics): Google's notice asks for non-commercial use. That is a request, not a licence term, and the previous set relied on the same kind of scan. The Politics scan is a HathiTrust download: its margins carry a HathiTrust stamp and Google's logo and need cropping, and it was uploaded to the Internet Archive by a private user.
- **The Teubner frame** is signed JVC, probably J. V. Cissarz (died 1942), so the design is public domain in the EU too.
- **Single books stand in for some titles.** Proclus is the *Elements of Theology*, not the commentaries or the *Platonic Theology*. The Golden Verses cover does not include Iamblichus; a 1918 abridged *Life of Pythagoras* (black cloth, gilt title, Commons PD-old-100-expired) is named in the JSON. The Phaedo volume's cover says "Plato and Aristotle".
- **Years from catalogue records, not the book:** Symposium (1905), Heraclitus (c. 1905, title page undated), Phaedrus (1901), Parmenides (1908).
