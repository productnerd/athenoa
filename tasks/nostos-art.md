## Worth a second look before approving

- **The Charioteer of Delphi** stands in for the chariot of the Phaedrus. Athena calls nous the charioteer (ἡνίοχος), but the statue is a single driver whose horses are lost, not the soul's winged pair. I found no ancient picture of the Phaedrus team on Commons.
- **Helios** stands in for Plato's Sun. Athena's Sun is a simile for the Good, and the vase shows the sun god himself.
- **The Tomb of the Diver** (Paestum) is offered for Diotima's plunge into the open sea and for the "Delian diver" Athena mentions for Heraclitus. It is the best-known Greek painting of such a dive, but it is a funeral image and illustrates neither.
- **Ter Brugghen's Heraclitus** shows the "weeping philosopher" of later tradition, while Athena calls Heraclitus the most luminous of them. The free paintings of Heraclitus on Commons are all of this weeping type.
- **Zeus or Poseidon** from Artemision: scholars still disagree which god it is.
- **Uncertain identities:** the Mantineia relief is traditionally taken to be Diotima, which is not certain. The Ostia head is Plotinus by an identification its Commons page calls "plausible but not proven". The "Hestia Giustiniani" is only traditionally named Hestia.
- **Orpheus krater:** the Met lists two large views (DT5486 and DT5487) without saying which side each shows. DT5486 comes first after the Met's main image, which shows the Orpheus side. Still, check that it shows Orpheus and not the libation scene on the back before downloading.
- **Aphrodite:** the Commons file name says swan; the bird is usually called a goose, and the caption says goose.

## Ideas with no good free image

- **Apollo and Dionysus shaking hands at Delphi.** Athena describes a particular vase with a palm tree between the two gods. It seems to be the calyx krater by the Kadmos Painter in the Hermitage, St Petersburg (inv. 1807, about 400 BC), which some scholars set on Delos rather than Delphi. It is not on Wikimedia Commons, and I found no other copy under a free licence. Nothing else on Commons shows the two gods together like this. The Dionysus cup and the Apollo kylix could be shown side by side instead.
- **The Titans dismembering Dionysus**, and the mirror they hold up to the child: I found no free picture on Commons.
- **Narcissus and the Orphic egg** are not in Athena's teaching as the content stands. Narcissus appears only as a wrong answer in one quiz item, and the only egg is in "chicken and egg". So neither has an entry.
- **"Know thyself" mosaic** (the skull with ΓΝΩΘΙ ΣΑΥΤΟΝ, Museo Nazionale Romano): the only clearly free copy on Commons is a 553px Alinari print. The larger photo comes from a commercial picture archive and its licence is unclear, so I left it out. The Delphi temple covers "Know thyself" instead.
- **Diagrams rather than artworks:** the divided line, the four terms of the Sun simile, the circles of the Same and the Other, the ennead and the tetraktys would be better drawn in the site's own line style.
- **Proclus** has no ancient portrait, and the head at Delphi sometimes called Plutarch is not securely him. I did not look for a photograph of **Kazantzakis**: any photo of him is from the twentieth century and would need its own copyright check.
- **The Eleusinian rites themselves** were secret, and few pictures of them exist. The Great Eleusinian Relief and the Persephone krater stand for them.
