# Nostos: reflection steps on the Learn path (proposal, 7 November 2026)

Maria's request: every two or three chapters of Learn, a self-reflection step. The learner takes a short quiz about themselves, the answers go to the AI, and the AI returns a personalised report, analysis or grouping (as in Fishbowl). Options per group of chapters, with a recommended pick (★).

## How each reflection would work (the same for all)

1. **The quiz, 8 to 12 items, a mix:** scenarios ("you are insulted in a meeting: what moves first?"), sliders (never ↔ always), ranking (drag to order), and one free-text line in your own words. About three minutes.
2. **A result that works for everyone:** each option has a **grouping** scored on the device (a type, a stage, a profile), so the step works even without the AI.
3. **The AI's reading, where Claude is on:** the answers (never the person's name) go to Claude with Athena's relevant teaching from the transcripts. Claude writes:
   - a short personal portrait in Athena's terms;
   - what is strong and what is "the shadow";
   - one practice from Athena's 173 practices for the coming week;
   - the stars on the map and the talk, with its minute, to go to next.

   It uses the same Claude connection as the tutor, Socratic sparring and teach-it-back.
4. **Kept, and compared later:** results stay in the browser, like Threads. Each one can be retaken, and the page shows how you have moved since last time (for example "three months ago: Thymos at the reins; now: the charioteer holds both"). The last step reads all of them together.
5. **Honest framing:** each says it is a reflection, not a psychological test or diagnosis. The ones touching love and death are worded gently and point to real help where relevant.

## The options

There are 15 reflection points across the 34 chapters: one in the middle of each longer stage and one at the end of each stage.

### 1. After chapters 1–2: What Philosophy Is · Socrates, the Model
- ★ **Which of Socrates' companions are you?** Questions on how you meet a hard question (defend, collect definitions, get bored, grow curious, feel "pregnant" with an idea).
  - Grouping: Euthyphro (certain he knows), Meno (collector of definitions), Alcibiades (gifted, distracted), Theaetetus (pregnant with ideas), Socrates' own aporia.
  - AI: the moment in the dialogue where Socrates turned that person, and what it asks of you.
- **Lover or knower?** After Athena's "I'm not a knower, I'm a lover of truth". How you relate to knowledge.
  - Grouping: the Collector, the Debater, the Seeker, the Practitioner.
  - AI: which Socratic habit to start with (begin from not knowing, define before you discuss, "what use is it?").
- **The three grades of "I know that I do not know".** Self-placement on Athena's three grades of the Socratic saying.

### 2. After chapters 3–4: The Call to Awaken · Hellenism, a Living Path
- ★ **What woke you, and your Apollo name.** Questions on what brought you here (pain, crisis, love, curiosity, a search, your roots) and how you seek.
  - Grouping: one of Apollo's five names (Pythios, Delios, Phanaios, Ismenios, Leschenorios), because "the god appears according to our state" (Plutarch).
  - AI: your step, and the passage ahead of you (washing, the simple, the archer, music).
  - Retaken at reflection 8, it shows movement.
- **Lukewarm or on fire?** How much of your spiritual life is consumption and experiences, and how much is practice ("the new spiritual cave").
  - Grouping: the Tourist, the Collector of experiences, the Seeker, the Practitioner.
- **Your own golden thread.** Your roots (family, language, faith, places).
  - AI: your personal lineage map, joining your background to the golden thread Athena traces. Sankofa: look in your own root first.

### 3. After chapters 5–6: The Soul and Its Parts · Mind and the True Self
- ★ **The charioteer.** Scenarios (a deadline against a party, an insult, a temptation, a hard truth) show which part of Plato's soul leads: appetite, spirit (thymos) or reason.
  - Grouping: who holds the reins, for example "the black horse leads", "thymos at the reins", "the charioteer asleep", "both horses pulling one way".
  - AI: your portrait, and Athena's daily self-observation questions adapted to your pattern.
- **Who plays the roles?** List your roles and labels and slide how attached you are to each.
  - Grouping: your "wardrobe" (the roles you wear most tightly).
  - AI: which one to loosen first (Athena's "loosen your labels").

### 4. After chapters 7–9: Being and Becoming · The Senses and the Calm Mind · Forgetting and Remembering
- ★ **Same and Other.** Questions on calm, reactivity, moods and what pulls you outward.
  - Grouping: Timaeus' two circles, as Same-led, Other-led, an inner equinox, or spinning.
  - AI: a portrait, and Athena's calm practices fitted to you (seek the calm, pure scents, guard what you listen to).
- **Your sensory diet.** An audit of what you take in daily: sounds, scents, images, news, food.
  - AI: sorts it into "rising" and "sinking" (Athena's terms) and proposes one week of purifying the senses. Very practical.
- **What have you forgotten?** Questions on childhood loves, moments of wonder and what you once knew without being taught.
  - AI: writes your "recollection": the thread of what your soul knew before (anamnesis).

### 5. After chapters 10–11: Living Well · Virtue and Character
- ★ **Your virtue map.** Scenarios test the four virtues (wisdom, courage, temperance, justice), each with its excess and its lack (Aristotle's mean, as Athena teaches it).
  - Grouping: a chart of where you tip, for example "courage tips to rashness", "temperance tips to rigidity".
  - AI: your two strongest virtues, the one to replant, and the habit to plant in its place.
- **Your own work (oikeion ergon).** Athena's two signs: does it make you burn with zeal, and do you lose the sense of time? Plus what makes you feel whole.
  - AI: the outline of the work your soul loves, and one small step this month.
- **The why chain.** Take three current choices; the AI asks "why?" until it reaches the root wish (Athena's chain to eudaimonia).
  - AI: your real telos, and whether your happiness rests on fortune's wheel or on your mast. This one is half quiz, half dialogue.

### 6. After chapters 12–13: Justice and Freedom · Paideia and the Polis
- ★ **Your inner republic.** What rules your decisions: wisdom, honour, money and security, every passing wish, or one obsession.
  - Grouping: the soul's regime in Plato's Republic (aristocracy, timocracy, oligarchy, democracy, tyranny), which is exactly Athena's psychopolitics: "the first polity we must learn to govern is our soul".
  - AI: your regime, how it shows, and one step of self-government.
- **Your noes and yeses.** List what you are refusing now.
  - AI: checks each «No» for its «Yes» and proposes what to build (Athena: "a No without a Yes is lame").
- **How far your circle reaches.** Your circle of concern, from yourself to family, town and world.
  - Grouping: idiotes or politikos.
  - AI: one act at your own scale.

### 7. After chapters 14–16: Eros (Desire, Eros and the Hero · Diotima's Ladder · Love That Binds the World)
- ★ **Where your eros rests.** What you love and how: bodies, souls, your city and its laws, knowledge, the arts, beauty itself; and whether your love is an ally of freedom or a grip.
  - Grouping: your rung on Diotima's ladder.
  - AI: the next rung, and how to "steer eros with the divine nous".
- **Ally or owner?** How you love the people close to you (control, jealousy, sacredness towards yourself).
  - Grouping: four postures of love.
  - AI: practices from Athena ("to love means: I am the ally of your freedom"). Worded with care.
- **The two Aphrodites.** Heavenly and common love in your life, as two dials.

### 8. After chapters 17–19: Apollo and the Oracle · Healing, Seeing and Aiming · Music and Harmony
- ★ **Your oracle.**
  - Retake your Apollo name from reflection 2, to see if you have moved.
  - Find which of Apollo's four powers you most need now: healing (cleansing), mantic seeing (silence), the archer (one aim), or music (harmony with others).
  - AI: chooses one of the Delphic maxims for you and writes a short oracle-style answer, with Athena's reading of it.
- **The silence test.** How you meet silence and inner listening.
  - AI: a listening practice for the week (Athena: "each of us is a seer").

### 9. After chapters 20–21: The Prisoners and the Shadows · Illusion and Its Makers
- ★ **Your cave inventory.** Give five things you are sure of (about a person, politics, yourself), and say what each rests on.
  - Grouping: where your convictions live on the Divided Line (conjecture, opinion, belief, reasoning, understanding).
  - AI: your "shadows" and your "wonder-workers" (the mind's narrators), with Athena's practices for catching conjectures and checking a conviction's emotional charge.
- **Who are your puppeteers?** Where your beliefs come from (news, feeds, family, school, people you admire).
  - AI: a map of the fire and the puppeteers in your cave, and one to turn away from.

### 10. After chapters 22–23: The Release and the Turn · The Sun and the Good
- ★ **Where are you in the cave?**
  - Grouping: one of the cave's seven stages (chained, freed, the steep ascent, starlight, daylight, the sun, the return), which are the practical guide's seven phases.
  - AI: your stage, that phase's gate behaviours from the guide, and what to protect now.
  - (Like everything here, framed as a map, not a ranking, as Athena warns.)
- **Your Good.** Rank what matters most to you.
  - AI: whether the Good is first or something else rules, and Athena's "fall in love with the Good".

### 11. After chapters 24–25: Heraclitus and the Ever-Living Fire · The One and Its Outflowing
- ★ **Wet soul or dry?** Heraclitus: "the dry soul is wisest and best". Your fears, angers, envies and attachments, as how sticky they are.
  - Grouping: soaked, damp, drying, dry flame.
  - AI: which passion to dry first, and how (Athena's "dry the passions").
- **Private or common logos?** How you meet disagreement: "my truth" or the shared Logos.
  - Grouping: the private thinker or the listener.
  - AI: a practice for dialogue.

### 12. After chapters 26–27: Non-Duality and Transcendence · Number, Time and the Solstices
- ★ **Your year.** Your energy across the year and your relation to the dark season.
  - AI: a personal calendar of Athena's four turnings: what to sow at the next equinox and what to bring down into the next solstice. It is dated, so in December it speaks of this winter's solstice.
- **Serious or playful?** How much of life you can hold as divine play.
  - Grouping: Athena's εὐθυμία, childlike cheer, as a dial.
  - AI: one way to let more play in.

### 13. After chapters 28–29: Myth as Symbol · The Gods Within
- ★ **Which god rules your house?** The gods as powers of the soul, as Athena reads them: Zeus (mind), Ares (vital force and boundaries), Athena (wisdom ruling force), Dionysus (transformation), Hermes, Aphrodite, Hestia (the centre), Apollo.
  - Grouping: your ruling god and your neglected one.
  - AI: a portrait, the Orphic hymn to read, and the practice to go with it (for example Athena's daily Ares audit).
- **Your boundaries.** Athena's "our centre is wisdom, but our boundaries must be red".
  - AI: where your fence (ἕρκος) holds and where it leaks.

### 14. After chapters 30–31: Dying Before Death · The Mysteries and the Second Birth
- ★ **Read your life as a myth.** The myth decoder turned round.
  - Five questions about one turning point in your life.
  - AI: reads it as a Greek myth (Persephone's descent and return, Heracles at the crossroads, Odysseus among the dead, Orpheus looking back) in Athena's way, and places you on the stages of the second birth (conception, gestation, labour, nourishment, the sun).
- **What would you die from?** What you would hate most to lose: body image, roles, possessions, beliefs.
  - AI: the identifications that hold you most, and a week of "die a little every day", one each day. Worded gently.
- **Your second birth.** Self-placement on the five stages alone.

### 15. After chapters 32–34: The Return Home (The Way Home to Ithaca · Hellenic Meditation · Living It Now)
- ★ **Your Odyssey (the journey report).** The capstone, which reads every earlier reflection, your Threads and your answers together.
  - AI: writes your Odyssey: where you set out, what moved and what didn't, the "suitors" still eating in your house, what your Ithaca is, and your work (ergon) in the world.
  - It ends with which reflections to retake.
- **Your suitors.** What occupies your house and eats your resources (habits, people, thoughts).
  - AI: a list and a plan to clear them, Odysseus' way.
- **Which phase are you in?** A placement on the practical guide's seven phases, with that phase's gate.

## What each one is for: the outcome, not just the theme (added 7 November 2026)

Maria asked whether these are just curiosity, and what the learner actually gets. They should not be curiosity. Each one ends the way a nutrition plan does: a small, specific prescription, chosen from Athena's 173 practices (Claude picks and fits them, it does not invent them), with a check-in.

**The plan at the end of every reflection, always the same shape:**
- **Add:** one practice, with its dose (when, how long, how often).
- **Cut:** one thing to reduce or stop.
- **Watch:** one cue to notice during the day.
- **Check in after 7 days:** two questions (did you do it, what changed).
- **Retake** after 4 to 6 weeks, to see the movement.

**What you learn:** the chapter's idea, applied to yourself. That is Athena's point that knowledge must be embodied: you understand the tripartite soul once you have seen which part drives your own Tuesday.

**Honest notes:**
- A few of the options in each group lean towards curiosity (a type that is fun to get). They are only worth building if they end in a plan like the ones above. The ★ picks were chosen because they do.
- The strongest for actually changing behaviour are #3, #5, #6, #9 and #11: they find a habit and replant it. #2, #8, #10 and #15 are the ones that show progress over time. #12 and #14 are the most personal and memorable.
- Research note (from the AI-in-education research now running): learners often feel they have learnt more than they have, and the plans work best when they are small and checked. That is why each has a 7-day check-in and a retake.
