**18 of 18 books have a free image. Total download: 12.3 MB** at about 1200px wide.

- All 18 come from the first printed edition of the Greek text (or, for Heraclitus and Parmenides, the first printed collection of their fragments). A few carry a caveat, listed below.
- **No licence is non-commercial.** The Bayerische Staatsbibliothek scans were expected to be CC BY-NC-SA, but each one's own IIIF manifest says Public Domain Mark 1.0. e-rara (Plotinus) is Public Domain Mark but asks for a source credit, given in the JSON. ## No free image, or only a later or partial edition

- **Golden Verses and Iamblichus** (`pythagorean`): the image covers the Golden Verses only (first Greek printing, Aldus 1495). Iamblichus' *Life of Pythagoras* was first printed in 1598 (ed. Arcerius), and I found no free scan of it. The only free image is a page of a 14th-century manuscript in the Laurenziana (Commons, `Iamblichos, De vita Pythagorica.jpg`, public domain, 824 KB).
- **Proclus** (`proclus`): the 1534 Basel edition is the first printing of his commentaries on the *Timaeus* and the *Republic*, but its title page is Plato's, with Proclus named as the addition. The *Platonic Theology* came later (Hamburg, 1618). A good 600 ppi scan of that is on the Internet Archive, marked Public Domain Mark, if a Proclus-only title page is preferred.
- **Heraclitus and Parmenides** (`heraclitus`, `parmenides`): their words survive only as quotations in later authors, so there is no editio princeps in the strict sense. Estienne's 1573 anthology is the first printed collection of them. Both pages come from the same book, so they share a look.
- **Plotinus** (`plotinus`): shown is the first Greek edition (1580). The very first printing was Ficino's Latin translation (Florence, 1492); I found no free full-page image of it. Likewise I used the 1513 Greek Plato, not Ficino's earlier Latin Plato (1484), which I did not look for.

## Worth a second look before approving

- **Eight pages were found by catalogue and OCR text, not by eye**, because I could not open the images: Odyssey, Orphic Hymns, Golden Verses, Ethics, Politics (BSB OCR), and Heraclitus, Parmenides and Proclus (Internet Archive OCR). The OCR does read the right headings on each page (quoted in the JSON notes), but a glance at each page link before downloading is wise.
- **Golden Verses**: the verses start halfway down the page; the top half is the end of the Gospel of John, which this grammar book also prints. The facing Latin page and the title page are named in the JSON note as alternatives.
- **Aristotle**: the BSB copy has two portraits of Piero Vettori bound in, and handwritten notes in the margins, so the Ethics and Politics pages may be annotated.
- **Odyssey date**: the colophon says 9 December 1488, but the catalogues add that it was finished no earlier than 13 January 1489, and the Commons pages of this edition say 1489. The JSON keeps 1488.
- **Parmenides**: `library.json` has Parmenides' poem, so that is what I found. If Plato's dialogue *Parmenides* was meant, Commons has its 1513 first page too (public domain, 1.2 MB).
- **Plato pages**: the Commons pages say only "editio princeps, Venice 1513" and do not name the library whose copy was scanned. The printer (Aldus with Andrea Torresani) comes from another copy's Internet Archive record.
- **Other options seen and not chosen**: a CC BY-SA photo of the Bodmer Odyssey open at Book XIII, the return to Ithaca (a display-case photo of a spread); two BnF pages of the 1488 Homer, public domain on Commons but credited "© BnF" by the BnF, so unclear; a CC BY-SA photo of the 1513 Plato title page from the Biblioteca de Murcia.
